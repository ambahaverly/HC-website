Guildford Borough Council

Weyside Urban Village

£467mregeneration programme delivering around 1,500 new homes

Scheme
1,500 new homes, community facilities and a new council depot on a Thames Water site
Our role
Finance lead, replacing a Big Four firm
Funding
Housing Infrastructure Fund, Enterprise M3 LEP and discounted PWLB borrowing

Guildford Borough Council brought us in to replace a Big Four firm as finance lead for its Weyside Urban Village regeneration programme.

The programme sought to deliver around 1,500 new homes, new community facilities and a new council depot on a Thames Water site, with a total cost of £467m.

We produced the development appraisal, assessed the project's viability, and identified the funding and disposal solutions needed to make it deliverable.

What we did

  • Development appraisal and viability assessment for the whole programme
  • Funding and disposal strategy
  • Housing Infrastructure Fund (HIF) application, grant claims and ongoing monitoring
  • Enterprise M3 LEP funding application and monitoring
  • Access to discounted PWLB borrowing
  • Ongoing financial modelling and cashflow management

Waverley Borough Council

Appraisals for new council homes

Independent appraisals that move housing schemes forward

Work
Development appraisals and acquisition appraisals
Focus
Hurdle rates, default values and best-practice benchmarking
Outcome
Stronger investment business cases

We provide Waverley with detailed, independent development appraisals that strengthen the business case for delivering new homes on council-owned sites.

Each appraisal tests the scheme's hurdle rates and default values and benchmarks them against best practice, so decisions rest on assumptions that stand up to scrutiny.

Our independent reports have strengthened the Council's business cases for investment, helping it advance the delivery of new homes and appraise acquisition opportunities with confidence.

What we did

  • Detailed development appraisals for council-owned sites
  • Assessment of hurdle rates and default values
  • Benchmarking against best practice
  • Independent appraisal of acquisition opportunities

Portsmouth City Council · One Public Estate

From housing depot to new homes

Making a challenging site viable

Commissioned by
Portsmouth City Council, through One Public Estate
Site
Gosport Borough Council's former housing depot
Challenge
Difficult local development viability

Gosport faces particular challenges around development viability. In a One Public Estate commission from Portsmouth City Council, we helped Gosport Borough Council find new ways to turn its old housing depot into new homes.

At the time, Portsmouth City Council's management team also ran Gosport Borough Council, so the commission came through Portsmouth.

Conventional approaches would not make the scheme stack up, so the Council needed to consider innovative ways to achieve development.

Working through the One Public Estate programme, we identified options that made the redevelopment of the old housing depot into new homes a viable reality.

What we did

  • Viability analysis of the depot site
  • Identification of innovative delivery and funding options
  • Options to overcome viability barriers

Westminster City Council

Institutional funding for temporary accommodation

368homes acquired that were previously leased as temporary accommodation

Term
42 years, index-linked
Retrofit
£33.5m embedded in the structure
Outcome
Assets unencumbered at lease expiry

A 42-year institutional funding solution that enabled Westminster to acquire 368 homes it previously leased for temporary accommodation.

Volatile nightly accommodation costs were replaced with structured, index-linked payments, giving the Council long-term control of supply and unencumbered ownership of the homes when the lease ends.

The structure also embedded £33.5m of retrofit funding to improve the homes.

What we did

  • Led the funding structure
  • Financial modelling and appraisal
  • Retrofit funding built into the deal

City of Westminster Pension Fund

Pension fund sale and leaseback

£50mcapital released from existing temporary accommodation stock

Term
40 years, index-linked
Portfolio
124 properties via a council-owned LLP
Outcome
Receipt reinvested in new TA supply

A £50m sale and leaseback between Westminster City Council and its own pension fund, releasing capital from 124 temporary accommodation properties.

The Council sold and leased back 124 existing TA properties to a council-owned LLP, funded by a 40-year, index-linked loan from the Pension Fund that matches the fund's own long-term liabilities.

The capital receipt can be reinvested in additional TA supply, while the Council keeps full use of the properties under the leaseback.

What we did

  • Designed and led the funding structure
  • Council-owned LLP arrangement
  • Matched the fund's liability profile
